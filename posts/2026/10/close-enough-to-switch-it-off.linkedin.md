While I was studying COBIT I came across a case study from 2016. The network of the operators who run the electricity transmission of Europe, forty-two of them at the time, from thirty-five countries, was putting its IT under the framework. And there is one sentence in it that I could not get past: the dashboard is completely managed in a single Excel file.

I should be fair to them. It is not the grid that sits in that file. It is the dashboard of their IT governance: which process is where, which actions are open, who follows up. But I stopped at that sentence anyway. A single Excel file.

For years we have lived with the assumption that platforms were created to do the work for us. Fewer people doing the job, because we are convinced, sometimes more than business continuity would tell us, that it is a winnable bet. And then you look at how the evidence for the security frameworks is actually kept, and a lot of it is Excel files.

My first reaction is that it is an outdated mindset. And it really is an issue when the job is to switch off a system to protect the infrastructure.

In the compliance space, I would assume, you either do it wrong and easy, or correct and really difficult sometimes. The Excel file, I think, is what people reach for when correct has become too difficult. Everybody already knows how to open it.

Now we talk more and more about AI monitoring, and we are still defining the roles: human in the loop, human validation, scope shifting. I have started to read those names as distances: how far the person stands from the thing that is running.

Human oversight is a distance before it is a role. It is the number of steps between the person who feels that something is going off and the switch, and whether that person, when they get there, is allowed to pull it.

Wrote about it with a small game. Five systems on one sheet, with a switch on every row, and you stand on one row. On your row you read the number live and the switch is one press away. Every other row is only a light, and the further the row, the older the light. You can walk, or you can send a request that is slow and decided on an old light. At the end, the game tells you how far away you were standing: https://arra.blog/posts/2026/10/close-enough-to-switch-it-off.html
