A feature that took over a month to build came back with one verdict: it makes no sense. So you reach for the cut — and find the thing has roots deeper than anything you can actually trace.

You trace it. You cut a clean leaf. A hidden reference you never saw springs up somewhere below. The root was always deeper than the part you could reach.

𝐂𝐮𝐭 𝐚 𝐥𝐞𝐚𝐟. 𝐀 𝐧𝐞𝐰 𝐫𝐨𝐨𝐭 𝐬𝐩𝐫𝐢𝐧𝐠𝐬 𝐮𝐩. 𝐓𝐡𝐞 𝐝𝐞𝐛𝐭 𝐤𝐞𝐞𝐩𝐬 𝐩𝐢𝐥𝐢𝐧𝐠.

So starting over starts to feel like sanity returning — a fresh app with only the features you want. But the rewrite carries its own roots you just cannot see yet. Start over or cut it out, the debt does not reset either way. It follows you, and it grows for having been deferred.

Neither one is a decision you can hand off. Somebody has to sit with the reference they could not trace and decide whether it stays or goes. That is the same instinct the EU put law behind — human oversight, because a model cannot be held accountable for what it reaches into. The human stays in the loop to decide what to keep, what to cut, and what was never the machine's call in the first place.

Wrote about it with a small game where a codebase is a tree of roots, you cut clean from the leaves up, a DEBT meter climbs while the half-cut code sits exposed, and a tempting REWRITE button wipes the tangle for a fresh start — except it never lowers your debt, it only adds to it: https://arra.blog/posts/2026/07/start-over-or-cut-it-out.html
