In my room at home there is a very old phone with a dial. Pale yellow. Last week, while I was getting my keys to pick up my daughter from pre-kindergarten, I took a long look at it for the first time in years.

Around the dial there are labels, in French: renseignements, réclamations, télégraphe, police-secours, pompiers. Information, complaints, telegraph, emergency police, the fire brigade. And one that says: regional services, see the directory.

Somebody sat down and decided which six things deserve to be written on the phone itself. Not your friend's number. Numbers that are not often in your life, but are there when you need them. A lesson learned from history, turned into a label.

The same week, two companies heading for an IPO came out, as if coordinated, to say AI development should slow down. I read Chomsky when I was young: media works by making separation with dichotomy labels. Good or bad. Slow or fast. Tamer or prey. Those labels get written before anything happens, to keep the conversation going and the spending going.

I spend between one and two hundred dollars a month on AI. I once went looking in the usage for the train of thought behind an answers. There is nothing there. Session ids, a key id. That is the whole receipt.

Then, on an evening walk that got dark faster than I expected, I asked myself what I would do if a dragon came out from the left. Not what it would do to me. Whether I would think of myself as its tamer or its prey. Neither word was going to help. The only useful thought was: which way is the road, and who do I call.

Human oversight is the part in between. It is the person who stands there before the label exists, when nobody has decided yet which number you dial for this, and who has to go and look anyway. It is not heroic. It is walking back down a dark trail, checking that what came back is what you asked for, and writing it down so someone else gets a label out of it.

Wrote about it with a small game. A rotary phone with five labels and a blank sixth one. Smoke from the kitchen, a stranger at the door, a wrong bill: dial the right service. Then the machine starts doing things, and no service has a number for that. Only the blank label works, and it means going to look yourself.
