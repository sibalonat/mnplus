A theater actor told a documentary crew that his real skill isn't memorizing the play. It's forgetting the last one fast enough to make room for the next.

Remember long enough to perform. Forget fast enough to keep going. The two pull against each other, and you need both.

I learned it the hard way. I tried to make one feature 100% complete — holding every requirement and edge case in my head at once. A month later it was stuck at 80%, and the bottleneck wasn't the model. It was me, refusing to put anything down.

The LLM is the better memory — it recalls an entire codebase on command. What it can't do is decide what's worth keeping, or what a compliance rule says we're even allowed to keep. We're supposed to be the better forgetting. That call is still ours.

Wrote about it with a small game where facts arrive one at a time and you choose: HOLD it or LET GO. Only four memory slots, and what you hold fades on its own — keep everything and it overflows, keep nothing and the recall finds an empty shelf: https://arra.blog/posts/2026/06/remember-long-enough-forget-fast-enough.html
